# Terrain-first adventure review

The campaign should feel like travelling through a place, rather than hopping between small floating platforms. Preserve the clean backgrounds, clear hazards and limited visual palette. Make the terrain carry the character of each world.

## Working design rules

- Use three to six substantial landforms as the foundation of a map. Small ledges should connect these, not constitute the whole landscape.
- Treat roughly 25–40% solid terrain as a useful starting range, not a quota. Protect the air space needed for building and jumping, sightlines to switches, and access to lower routes.
- Medium adventures should have two or three distinct phases: prepare a route, navigate a crossing or descent, and solve the final approach. Extend the journey through space, not by requiring repetitive clicks.
- Keep the first five approachable. Larger terrain does not have to mean more difficult puzzles.
- Later maps can scroll vertically. Reveal the route below gradually, but let players inspect the whole map before starting.
- Every large solid region should offer something to do: tunnel, mine, climb, bridge around, or use as a safe holding area. Avoid decorative solid masses outside the route.
- Do not uniformly scale existing maps. That changes jump lengths, fatal drops, build budgets, object timing and trap clearances.

## Review of all 20 existing maps

Occupancy is the percentage of the current collision grid that is solid terrain, measured before play. It is a diagnostic, not a quality score. Changes below are proposals; the existing campaign is not modified by this review.

## Implementation order

1. Review the oversized sweet previews to establish the preferred visual scale.
2. Rebuild the lowest-density maps first: 4, 6, 9 and 10; then 7 and 13.
3. Consolidate circus terrain without adding more background items.
4. Refine the already substantial forest and mountain maps, preserving their stronger puzzles.
5. For each changed map, verify a scout solution within stock, safe crowd passage, object alignment, fall distances and star time. Keep difficulty tied to decisions rather than length alone.

## Sweet preview changes

The five additional medium previews now use 850-pixel-tall worlds, large packets and rolls, and deeper terrain bodies. The first two traverse both upper and lower tiers in opposite orders. The third uses an entire packet as an excavatable mountain; the fourth climbs a necklace and giant lolly formation; the fifth doubles back beneath an oversized ridge and opens a rescue chute for the crowd.

These are local review changes, not a published campaign expansion.
